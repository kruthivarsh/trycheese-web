If you shoot on Android and you've watched iPhone friends post photos from film camera apps like mood.camera and Lampa, you've probably gone looking for the same thing on the Play Store. In October 2026 the honest answer is: the most talked-about film camera apps are still iPhone-only, and Android has far fewer options built the same way.

This post is a snapshot of where things stand, what actually makes a **film camera app for Android** worth installing, and what to look for while the gap closes.

## Why the best-known film camera apps skipped Android

Two of the most praised film camera apps of the last couple of years don't ship on Android at all. Lampa's site describes itself as an iPhone camera and offers a single "Download for iOS" button. mood.camera is explicit about it in its FAQ, citing "the added complexity of the Android eco-system" and a single developer's limited time.

That reasoning is fair. Android runs on hundreds of phone models with different sensors, camera stacks and GPU drivers, so a processing pipeline that has to look identical on every phone is far more work than one tuned for a handful of iPhones. The result, though, is that Android shooters who want the same capture-time film look have mostly been left with filter apps.

## Film camera app vs film filter app

They look similar in screenshots, but they work differently:

- **A filter app** restyles a photo after the fact. It usually starts from the phone's own JPEG — already sharpened, HDR-blended and noise-reduced — and paints a look on top.
- **A film camera app** makes the look part of the capture. Colour, grain and halation are rendered into the photo at the shutter, and there's typically no live filter preview and no editing step afterwards. You shoot, and the photo comes out finished.

That second approach is the one we explain on the trycheese homepage under [why there's no live preview](/#in-the-moment): the effects are too heavy to render accurately in real time, and not seeing them keeps you looking at the scene instead of the screen.

## What's available on Android right now

The clearest new arrival this year is **ELMAR**, an Android-only film camera app that launched on Product Hunt on 2 May 2026. Its listing names film simulations inspired by Kodak Portra, Fuji Pro 400H, Velvia and Cinestill 800T (seven looks in total), a 28/35/50/75mm focal length selector, a lens effect modelled on the Leica Elmar 50mm, and a scan mode that inverts real developed negatives through the phone's camera. It's a good sign: developers are starting to treat Android as a first-class film camera platform rather than an afterthought.

Beyond that, most of what you'll find by searching "film camera" on the Play Store sits in the filter-app category described above. That isn't a criticism of those apps — they're useful — but if what you want is the iPhone-style "it's already film" experience, check the listing carefully before you install.

## Why film looks on a phone at all

Part of the appeal is cost. Analog.Cafe's Film Price Report for Q3 2026 found prices across 37 tracked film stocks up 2.5% since January, with film now 6% more expensive in Europe and Canada, and a 36-exposure roll of Kodak Gold 200 averaging $10.34. Real film is stable in price but not cheap, and that's before developing and scanning.

A good phone film look isn't a replacement for shooting real film photography — it's a way to get some of that character every day, on the camera you already carry.

## What to look for in an Android film camera app

Whatever you install, these are the things that separate a convincing look from a sticker:

1. **Processing at capture, not afterwards.** The look should be baked into the photo, not a preset applied to an already-processed image.
2. **Grain that's rendered, not overlaid.** Real film grain varies with brightness and shouldn't repeat as a visible pattern.
3. **Halation only where it belongs.** On film, the red-orange glow appears around bright light sources. An app that glows every edge is faking it.
4. **A filmic tone curve.** Highlights should roll off softly instead of clipping to white.
5. **Real camera controls.** Exposure compensation, lens choice and aspect ratios matter more than a hundred presets.
6. **Clear privacy.** You should know whether your photos ever leave the phone.
